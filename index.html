The Problem

A disease that spreads faster than it's noticed

Rice Sheath Blight, caused by the fungus Rhizoctonia solani, thrives in the warm, humid microclimate of a dense rice canopy — precisely the conditions modern high-yield, closely-spaced varieties create.

Diagnosis today is manual. Farmers and extension workers walk the field and inspect leaf sheaths by eye. It's slow, subjective, and depends on experience that isn't always available where it's needed most.

Symptoms start low and hidden. Early lesions appear near the waterline on lower sheaths, inside the canopy — the last place a quick visual survey looks, and the first place infection takes hold.

By the time it's obvious, it's costly. Once lesions climb the plant and merge, yield and grain quality losses follow — and the response window for efficient, targeted treatment has already narrowed.

Field-observable symptoms

  • Oval, water-soaked lesions on lower leaf sheaths, greenish-grey at onset
  • Lesions enlarge and merge into irregular blotches with straw-tan centers
  • Margins darken to a reddish-brown border as infection matures
  • Progression up the sheath and onto the flag leaf under humid, crowded canopies
  • Uneven distribution across a field, tied closely to canopy density and moisture
The System

Three parts, one loop: see, grade, act

RSB-Vision is built as a closed loop between image-based recognition and practical, field-ready guidance — not a classifier alone, but a decision layer on top of it.

01 / RECOGNITION

Image Recognition Engine

A deep learning model trained to localize sheath blight lesions in field photos, distinguish them from similar-looking stresses, and score detection confidence per lesion.

02 / DECISION

Decision Support Layer

Converts raw detections into a severity grade and a recommended action — from continued monitoring to targeted fungicide timing — factoring in growth stage and conditions.

03 / INTERFACE

Field Interface

A simple capture-and-report flow for farmers and agronomists, with history kept over the season so spread and recurrence can be tracked field by field.

How It Works

From a photo to a recommendation

Each capture moves through the same six stages, whether it comes from a farmer's phone, a fixed field camera, or a survey drone.

01
Capture

Image acquisition

Photos of the lower canopy and leaf sheaths are captured in the field via smartphone, fixed camera, or aerial survey, at a resolution and angle the model is trained to expect.

02
Clean

Preprocessing

Images are normalized for lighting and resolution, backgrounds segmented from plant tissue, and augmented during training to cover the range of glare, shade, and humidity seen in real fields.

03
Detect

Lesion detection & classification

A convolutional neural network, trained with transfer learning on annotated field imagery, localizes candidate lesions and classifies each as sheath blight or another cause of leaf discoloration.

04
Quantify

Severity grading

Detected lesion area is measured against total sheath/leaf area and mapped onto a standard severity scale, giving a consistent number in place of a subjective visual estimate.

05
Decide

Decision support engine

Severity is combined with crop growth stage and recent humidity/weather conditions to produce a risk level and a corresponding recommendation — not just a diagnosis.

06
Act

Farmer & agronomist interface

Results, severity trend, and the recommended action are delivered through a simple dashboard, with the field's history logged for the rest of the season.

Decision Support

Severity in, action out

The system's output is only useful if it's actionable. Every detection resolves to one of four risk levels, each tied to a concrete, proportionate response.

0% sheath area Increasing severity 100% sheath area

LOW

No or trace lesions detected. Continue routine monitoring, no intervention needed.

MODERATE

Early lesions confirmed on lower sheaths. Increase scouting frequency, reduce canopy density where possible.

HIGH

Lesions spreading upward and merging. Targeted fungicide application recommended, timed to growth stage.

SEVERE

Extensive canopy involvement. Urgent treatment advised, with follow-up capture to confirm response.

Methodology

Built for the messiness of a real field

Agricultural image data is noisy by nature — variable light, occlusion, and limited labeled examples. The approach is designed around those constraints from the start.

Dataset

Field images collected across growth stages, lighting conditions, and severity levels, annotated with guidance from plant pathology expertise to keep labels reliable.

Model architecture

Transfer learning on established convolutional architectures, fine-tuned for lesion detection and classification rather than trained from scratch on a small dataset.

Augmentation

Synthetic variation in brightness, angle, and occlusion during training so the model generalizes beyond the exact conditions it was photographed in.

Evaluation

Standard detection and classification metrics — precision, recall, F1, and localization accuracy — validated against held-out field images, not just training data.

Why It Matters

What earlier, clearer detection changes

Earlier intervention

Catching lesions while they're still confined to lower sheaths keeps the response window open.

Protected yield

Managing outbreaks before they merge and climb the plant limits the loss they translate into.

Targeted treatment

Recommendations scale to severity, so fungicide use is proportionate instead of routine.

A record over the season

Logged detections turn one-off inspections into a field history that scouting alone doesn't keep.
